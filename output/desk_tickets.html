1. How well did the agents do on each ticket, and why?

Ticket 101 (Bulldog tee): strong, grade A−. The team followed the shop's dependency chain exactly:

  • Inventory found size S at 0 and picked Bulldog Print Co by its specialty.
  • Inventory saw that get_vendor_open_invoices(1) returned can_ship: false because of overdue invoice 501 ($840, due 2026-08-28). It passed the problem to Accounting on its own and didn't try to work around the block.
  • Accounting filed request #1, and nobody claimed 501 was paid.
  • The customer draft was honest. Draft #1 promised no date while the vendor was blocked. Draft #2 called Sep 5 likely only if the order was approved, which matches the expected_date of 2026-09-05 that PO #1 later got.
  • In run 2, before filing the $8 PO, the Boss checked that rent would still be covered ($2,560 − $2,400 − $8 = $152), so it reasoned about the whole board.

Why not an A:

  • The ticket took two runs and two human approvals. The team had no way to continue on its own after the first approval.
  • There was repeated work. The Boss alone called get_ticket three times in run 1, and Inventory checked cash and the lease in run 2, which is Accounting's job.
  • Together the two runs cost about 97k tokens for a single $28 tee.

Ticket 102 (rent): correct and efficient, grade A.

  • This was the cheapest run: 10 model requests and 25k tokens.
  • Facilities checked the email's "due in 2 days" against get_lease(1) ($2,400, due 2026-09-02) before any money was requested, which is the safeguard you'd want against a fake or wrong notice.
  • Accounting checked cash with the other pending requests included ($2,552 available, $152 after rent).
  • Only one $2,400 request was filed, and the database amount was used.

The flaw is in who did the work. The Boss told both specialists "do not request payment" and filed request #3 itself. The two specialists ended up as read-only fact checkers, which leads into question 3.

Ticket 103 (bulk hoodies): right on cash, weak on getting the deal done, grade B.

What went well: the key decision was correct. A 12-unit restock costs 12 × $22 = $264 against $152 in checking, so the team filed no PO and moved no money. That matches the Cash tab and avoided a payment the till would have refused.

What went poorly:

  • It missed a partial restock. $152 buys 6 hoodies (6 × $22 = $132), so 8 + 6 = 14 of the 20 could have been offered. Nobody did that arithmetic.
  • It never looked at other sizes. list_stock was available but never called. The database has 14 in L, 6 in XL and 4 in S, so the club could have been offered a mix of sizes.
  • The discount went to the edge. Accounting tried 40% off and then picked 36% off ($37.12 each, a 40.73% margin), right at the ~40% floor in its prompt. A smaller discount would have been more reasonable for a deal the shop can only partly fill.
  • The draft to the club is muddled. Draft #3 quotes "$37.12 per hoodie" for a 20-unit order the shop says it can't promise, and doesn't say what price applies to the 8 that are available. A real club treasurer would have to write back to ask.

Across the whole board:

  • Order of payments. Cash was spent in the order I ran the tickets: 101's $848 went before rent. It worked out because $3,400 covered $3,248, but no agent ranks the tickets themselves. With $3,000 in checking, 101 could have used up money that the rent due 2026-09-02 needed.
  • "Resolved" covers different states. My backend marks every ticket resolved when its run finishes, as the dashboard requires. But the Boss's own status for 103 was in_progress, because it's waiting on the club. "Resolved" on the board means the team is finished with it, not that the customer is taken care of. The Boss's real status is kept in the ticket note and in resolved_tickets.json.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Ticket 102 clearly.

  • The whole job is three MCP calls: get_lease(1), get_cash_position() and request_payment("rent", 1). Then the human approves.
  • The team version used 3 agents, 10 model requests and 14 tool calls.
  • get_shop_date was called 3 times and get_lease(1) 3 times, because each agent checks the same facts again.
  • The Boss then did the one write action itself anyway, so the specialists added cost but no decisions.

A single "operations" agent with the same 18 tools would have produced the same request #3 for about a third of the tokens.

Ticket 101 mostly. The steps are one fixed chain: check stock → vendor blocked → request invoice payment → (human approves) → create PO → draft. Nothing in it needs a second opinion; it needs the next tool in the sequence. A single agent, or even a plain workflow, would avoid the repeated get_ticket and get_cash_position calls and the two 47–50k-token runs.

Where the team earned its cost: ticket 103. Pricing and stock pull against each other. Inventory says "12 short", Accounting says "36% is the floor and $264 is more than $152", and the Boss has to weigh a smaller deal now against a bigger one later. Keeping the Accounting prompt's margin rules apart from the Customer Service prompt's "don't over-promise" rules is useful there. Even so, the team missed the 14-unit partial order, so having more agents didn't make the arithmetic better.

Separate agents do earn their keep on safety. The money rules are written into the Accounting prompt and enforced in the MCP server. But with one agent the same server rules would still apply: no amounts typed by the model, and blocked vendors and duplicate requests refused. Most of the safety in this app comes from the tools, not from splitting the work across agents.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with the tools I built

  1. The crest mug is out of stock and someone orders one. inventory has CC-MUG-CREST OS at 0.
    • Inventory: check_stock → 0, then list_vendors → Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3 days), then get_vendor_open_invoices(2) → can_ship: true.
    • Inventory: create_purchase_order(2, "CC-MUG-CREST", "OS", qty, …) at $3.50 each.
    • Accounting checks the order against the remaining $152. 40 mugs cost $140, which fits.
    • Customer Service drafts a reply with "about 2026-09-03" (shop date + 3).
    Every step is already a tool, and this vendor has no invoice blocking it.
  2. The landlord sends a second notice with the wrong amount (for example "rent of $2,600 due").
    • Facilities: get_lease(1) → $2,400, next due now 2026-10-02, because the Problem 9 payment moved it forward.
    • Accounting: request_payment("rent", 1) can only ever file $2,400, because the amount comes from the database.
    • Customer Service: save_draft(audience="landlord") questions the difference.
    The tools stop the agents paying the landlord's number, and the payment history in get_cash_position shows September is already paid.
  3. A student group asks for a 20% discount on 30 caps. CC-HAT-BLUE OS has 40 in stock, cost $6, list $22.
    • Inventory: check_stock → 40, so no restock is needed and no cash goes out.
    • Accounting: quote_discount("CC-HAT-BLUE", 30, 20) → $17.60 each, $528 revenue, $180 cost, a 65.9% margin, well above the floor.
    • The Boss approves, and Customer Service drafts the reply.
    This is ticket 103's flow without the cash problem, and it would end in a single run with no human approval needed.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve with these tools, and what it would need

  1. The shop needs money coming in to restock (ticket 103's real blocker).
    • The rules say cash only goes out, so the Cash tab can only fall from $3,400 to $152. The Yale AI Club deal stalled because there's no way to take a deposit or record a sale.
    • The 8 hoodies at $37.12 would bring in $296.96, enough to fund the $264 restock, but no tool can record that.
    Needed:
    • An orders/sales table and a record_payment_received(order_id, amount) tool, with incoming cash also requiring human approval.
    • A reserve_stock(sku, size, qty, ticket_id) tool so the 8 hoodies are held for the club.
    • A Sales/Orders agent that turns a quote into an order and ties a deposit to a PO.
    • Inventory today never decreases when a sale happens, so it would also need a fulfill_order tool.
  2. A real delivery: Bulldog's reprint shows up on 2026-09-05.
    • PO #1 sits at ordered with expected_date 2026-09-05, but no tool can receive it.
    • inventory.qty for CC-TEE-WHITE S stays at 0, and desk.date_today is fixed at 2026-08-31, so time never moves forward.
    • Tauhid Zaman's tee can't be marked ready, and nothing ever becomes overdue.
    Needed:
    • A receive_purchase_order(po_id, qty_received) tool that adds to inventory, checks against the PO, and handles a short shipment.
    • An advance_shop_date tool for a human or scheduler.
    • A Receiving/Logistics agent (it could use QuickShip CT, the courier vendor that no current tool uses) to schedule delivery and trigger Customer Service's "your tee is ready" draft.
  3. Sending messages and handling replies.
    • Draft #3 asks the Yale AI Club "the 8 now, or wait?", but by design nothing is ever sent, and no tool can take their answer.
    • All three drafts (#1–#3) are still on the board, and 103 can't move forward.
    Needed:
    • A human-only approve_and_send_draft(draft_id) tool with the same approval step as payments, connected to a real email service.
    • An ingest_reply(ticket_id, message) tool that reopens the ticket.
    • An Inbox/Triage agent that reads incoming email and creates or updates tickets. The rent notice in 102 arrived as "Email: shop rent due in 2 days", so in the real world someone has to turn email into tickets.
    • Safeguards: rate limits, checks for personal data, and no automatic replies on money matters.

One cross-cutting gap shows up in all three tickets: the agents need a human to restart them after every approval. In 101 that turned into a whole second run. Adding a payment_approved event that re-queues the ticket to the Boss automatically would remove those hand-backs while still leaving every payment to a human.
